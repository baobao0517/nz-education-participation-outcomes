# Executive Summary

**NZ Education Participation & Outcomes Insights**  
*Independent public-sector analytics portfolio · 11 November 2026*

## Decision question

Where are gaps in school attendance and educational outcomes persisting across New Zealand regions and learner groups, and which areas warrant closer investigation or targeted support?

### Evidence base

- **Participation:** official Education Counts Term 2 regular attendance, 2022–2026, all 16 Regional Councils. Regular attendance means attending more than 90% of the term. **2026** is the latest observed decision baseline.
- **Outcomes:** official school-leaver NCEA Level 2+ attainment, 2019–2025; **2025** is the latest school-leaver baseline. Student ethnicity, school EQI group and age at leaving are examined separately for 2025.
- **Integrated:** 2025 Term 2 attendance versus 2025 school-leaver L2+ attainment at Regional Council level. Metrics are not measured on identical student cohorts.

## Executive findings

**01 / Overall attendance recovery, but not yet target attainment.** The share of students attending more than 90% of Term 2 increased from **39.6% (2022)** to **64.3% (2026)**, or **+24.7 percentage points**. This remains **15.7 pp below** the government's 80% by 2030 target. Do not confuse the 35.7% outside the regular-attendance threshold with students absent for the full term.

**02 / Persistent gaps in both participation and outcomes.** Gisborne, Northland and Waikato remained below the *corresponding year's* NZ attendance level in each of **2022–2026**, and below corresponding NZ NCEA L2+ attainment in each of **2019–2025**. In 2026, Gisborne and Northland recorded attendance of **51.3% and 52.4%** respectively, versus **64.3%** nationally. In 2025, their L2+ rates were **68.5% and 72.1%**, versus **77.8%** nationally. Waikato's gaps are narrower but similarly persistent. These are priority locations for **diagnostic investigation**, not proof of programme failure.

**03 / Outcome differences require subgroup context.** Among 2025 attributable-region school leavers, **16.4%** left before age 17; this was **29.6% in Gisborne**, **27.1% in Northland** and **22.6% in Waikato**. Likewise, **17.8%** of attributable-region leavers were associated with schools classified in the **More-barriers EQI group**, compared with **40.7%** in Gisborne and **43.5%** in Northland. These are important compositional clues but do not establish individual socioeconomic or causal effects. Ethnic-group differences should be compared within the *same group* and with total-response definitions respected.

**04 / Mismatch cases identify different questions.** Tasman recorded attendance **3.0 pp above** the national 2025 benchmark but NCEA L2+ **5.0 pp below** it. Its L2+ rate decreased from **82.6% (2019)** to **72.8% (2025)**; among 2025 leavers in Moderate EQI schools it was **72.9% (n=573)**, below the **80.1%** pooled same-group regional comparator. Bay of Plenty's nominal +0.3 pp L2+ gap versus published NZ is highly sensitive to national geographical coverage, so it should **not** be labelled a proven positive outlier.

**05 / Recovery is a reason to evaluate practice.** Taranaki's Level 2+ rate increased from **72.9% in 2023** to **79.6% in 2025**. Manawatū–Whanganui's Term 2 attendance improved to **64.4% in 2026** (NZ **64.3%**) after lagging earlier. Neither observed change can be attributed to particular interventions without further evaluation.

## Practical priorities

1. **Gisborne + Northland:** map school-level and locality-specific attendance barriers, early leaving, health/transport access and existing school–whānau engagement; design support only once those needs are established.
2. **Waikato + Manawatū–Whanganui:** distinguish sustained but different trajectories, compare like-for-like schools and age/EQI composition, and monitor later NCEA releases before linking improvement stories.
3. **Tasman:** investigate the participation–attainment mismatch and whether changes in early leaving, pathways and school composition help explain it; use multi-year data and caution with small samples.
4. **Taranaki:** identify and independently evaluate promising implementation practices before transfer to other regions; a recovery trajectory is not evidence of programme causality.

## Caveats for decision makers

- Attendance and NCEA L2+ are **different populations and observation windows**; the 2025 quadrant is ecological and descriptive only.
- Official nationwide NCEA L2+ (**77.8%**) includes **6,045 region-unattributed leavers**, with a low attainment rate. The 16-region attributable pool (**81.1%**) is not an alternative published NZ figure; use it only for like-for-like subgroup diagnostics.
- 2025 attendance reporting rules and codes changed; school-leaver achievement requirements evolved during the period. Trends cannot isolate policy or COVID-19 effects.
- Avoid interpreting socioeconomic or ethnicity group summaries as measures of individual ability, school quality, or causal effects. Small n matters.
